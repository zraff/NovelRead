The red sun on the horizon signaled the end of the day, and the shopkeepers began lighting mana lamps to help them see in the growing darkness.

Weaving his way through the crowd, Sven headed straight toward the butcher at the other end of the market, while still keeping an eye out for any amulet that caught his attention.

"Mr. Hufferman!" he called out. "Got anything decent left?" His eyes were already fixed on the half-severed lamb hanging behind the counter.

The butcher looked up from wiping down his counter and grinned. "Of course, Master Sven. I have something decent for you." He reached for the lamb, cut off a piece, and started chopping it.

"Special occasion?" Hufferman asked. He hadn't seen Sven in a while, and he was aware of his circumstances.

"Nothing much. Just a reminder of times past for my mother."

"Oh, great. Give my best wishes to Ms. Kruse as well. Here's a little extra from me."

Hufferman packed the meat neatly in paper and handed it to Sven. "And come back often, will ya?"

"You didn't have to. Thanks. I might just do that." Sven handed him a one-moor note from his pocket, careful not to touch the bundle of notes he'd received earlier.

"Amazing! Solved your worries?" The butcher was surprised that Sven agreed so readily.

"Mostly."

"Well, good for you, then. You should take it easy sometimes."

The words caught Sven off guard for a moment, but he simply nodded and thanked the old man before heading toward the stall where he'd seen a shiny sparrow pin.

Near the beginning of the market was a magic item seller who also sold trinkets and amulets.

"How much for this?" Sven held the sparrow pin in his hand.

"Eight moors."

"She'll like this," he murmured to himself. He bought it even though he felt the shopkeeper had overpriced it a little.

He tucked the pin into the left-side pocket of his jacket and started walking home with a smile on his face.

"I don't know. My son keeps it. I don't know where he has..."

But unknowingly, her eyes flicked toward the floorboard beneath the dining table.

The man noticed.

He slapped her hard across the cheek, tied her limbs with rope, closed the door, and moved toward the table.

Shirin's cheek bled as she stared at the man. His head was covered with a cloth, leaving only his eyes visible.

The knife in his hand looked longer than when it had been held against her throat, almost the size of a crowbar. With it, he pried open the floorboard.

"Found it!"

Just as he approached Shirin again, possibly to silence her for good...
